# Averaged diagnostics live in snapshots, as raw sums

Quantities followed over time, such as kinetic energy and dissipation, go to the time series. Diagnostics meant to be averaged over a window, such as spectra, budget spectra, moments and PDFs, are written into the snapshots and averaged afterwards into a time-average file. A snapshot already sits at a sample time and holds the state they derive from, so a missing one can be recomputed, and the time series stays a small table to plot. What is stored is what averages linearly: raw moments $\langle f^p \rangle$ rather than skewness or flatness, and counts over bin edges fixed in advance rather than densities, so that a statistic is a plain mean over samples and standardized moments and densities are formed only after averaging. The earlier pipelines reread snapshot files with denominator bugs, and a time mean of instantaneous skewness is not the skewness of the window.
